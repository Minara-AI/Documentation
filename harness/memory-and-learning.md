# Memory and learning

Memory lets Minara carry useful context from one conversation to the next, so you don't have to repeat yourself.

## What Minara can remember

* Your preferences, such as the assets you follow or the level of risk you're comfortable with.
* Earlier research and the reasoning behind past decisions.
* Investment theses you're tracking.
* Lessons from past tasks that make future answers better.

This is more useful than a trade history alone. A trade history shows *what* happened; memory can also keep *why* a decision made sense at the time, and what happened afterwards.

## How Minara learns

Over time, Minara can turn useful experience into better working habits:

**Observe → Reflect → Distill → Reuse**

A profitable trade doesn't automatically mean the reasoning was right, and a losing trade doesn't automatically mean the idea was wrong. Minara looks at the reasoning, not just the outcome.

## What memory does not do

* **It is not permission.** A remembered preference or an earlier approval never counts as approval for a new trade. See [Safety and approvals](safety-and-approvals.md).
* **It does not replace fresh data.** Minara still checks current market and account information.
* **It only covers what Minara can see.** Trades or accounts outside Minara aren't included unless you've connected them.

## Manage your memories

You can see what Minara remembers and delete anything you don't want it to keep. See [Personalization](../features/personalization.md#memories).
